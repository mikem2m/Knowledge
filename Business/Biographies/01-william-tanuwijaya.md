# 01 - William Tanuwijaya

## 1. One-paragraph summary

William Tanuwijaya is a software engineer from a modest family in Pematang Siantar. He co-founded Tokopedia, an online marketplace, in 2009 with Leontinus Alpha Edison, using capital from his then-boss Victor Fungkong. Tokopedia charged almost nothing to list. It made money first from optional seller subscriptions and ads, and later from fees and payments. It grew by raising roughly US$3 billion of foreign venture capital over a decade instead of becoming profitable. In 2021 it merged with Gojek to form GoTo, which listed on the IDX in 2022. In 2024 TikTok took control of Tokopedia. William was a minority shareholder from the very beginning, so the story is less "boy builds empire" and more "operator builds a large asset that other people mostly own." For a reader whose goal is durable cash flow and real ownership, that contrast is the main lesson.

## 4. How the business actually makes money

- **Who pays, and for what:** Sellers pay, not buyers. Buyers use Tokopedia for free; payment fees and shipping are sometimes passed on. Sellers pay for visibility (TopAds), for status and tools (Gold Merchant, later Power Merchant and Official Store tiers), and later for transaction-based service fees. Fulfilment, payments and financial services were added later (TokoCabang warehousing from 2019, insurance, investment products).
- **Revenue model:** It's a pure marketplace. Tokopedia does not own inventory; sellers do. Revenue is roughly GTV (gross transaction value, the total value of goods sold) times the take rate (the percentage Tokopedia keeps). Until about 2015 the basic service was entirely free (reported, academic summaries of Tokopedia materials). Early monetization was Gold Merchant at about Rp100,000 per month. Fewer than 10% of roughly 10,000+ sellers paid at the time (reported, Kontan, about 2014).
- **Unit economics:** The key number is the take rate, and it was low.
  - FY2021: gross take rate about 1.9% and net about 0.9% (reported, Samuel Sekuritas, from GoTo filings). Another analyst calculated about 2.7% for FY2021 using a different definition, so the definitions are not consistent.
  - 1Q2022: about 2.9%, up from 2.5% a year earlier (confirmed, GoTo release via Antara).
  - FY2022: about 3.2% (reported, Momentum Works).
  - Comparison: in 2021, analysts put Sea at about 6.9% and Grab at about 5.7% (reported, Bisnis).
  - Worked example (inference): on a Rp100,000 order at a 1% net take rate, Tokopedia keeps Rp1,000. That has to cover payment processing, customer service, fraud losses, servers, any shipping subsidy and marketing. Any free-shipping promotion on that order loses money.
  - Gross vs net take rate: the gap is mostly incentives (cashback, vouchers, shipping subsidies) paid back to users (inference based on how GoTo reported "net revenue").
- **Cost structure and the main cost driver:** Main costs were (1) incentives and promotions to win buyers against Shopee, (2) marketing, including TV campaigns and Waktu Indonesia Belanja sale events, and (3) people, about 4,700 employees in 2019 (reported, Wikipedia). For scale, GoTo as a group lost about Rp7.6tn in the first 7 months of 2021 (confirmed, prospectus via Bisnis and Jakarta Post). It booked about Rp39.6tn of net loss in FY2022 (reported, Samuel Sekuritas). That FY2022 figure likely includes large non-cash goodwill impairment from the merger (inference; verify in the annual report).
- **Cash cycle:** This is the good part of the model. Tokopedia used a rekening bersama (escrow account): the buyer pays Tokopedia first, the seller ships, and Tokopedia releases the money after the buyer confirms receipt (confirmed, multiple academic descriptions). The platform therefore holds cash before it pays sellers. That gives it negative working capital (dana mengendap, or float), and it holds no inventory. The cash problem was never working capital; it was the operating loss from subsidies and headcount (inference). The founder's 2019 claim was that it could be profitable "if we stop expanding," and that all profit was reinvested (reported, Nikkei via 36kr). Treat that claim skeptically.
- **Distribution and customer acquisition:**
  - Supply first: about 70 sellers were signed before launch (reported).
  - Free press: Tempo coverage brought business-owner readers (reported).
  - SEO: sellers made topical products, for example #KamiTidakTakut t-shirts after the 2009 Jakarta bombings, which ranked well in search (reported).
  - Free listing pulled in small sellers (UMKM) who couldn't afford a website.
  - Later: national TV ads, sale events, and from 2015 Gojek drivers delivering Tokopedia orders (confirmed, Verdict).
- **Moat or unfair advantage:**
  - Early on: trust through escrow, in a market where Kaskus and Tokobagus were essentially classified-ad sites with no payment protection. Buyers there could transfer money and the seller could disappear (reported, William in Tempo).
  - Later: seller network effects. Millions of merchants, many of them first-time sellers, plus the habit of searching Tokopedia.
  - The moat was not price. Shopee showed that a well-funded entrant could buy share with subsidies (inference).
- **What is unknown here:**
  - Tokopedia's standalone P&L before 2021 (it was private).
  - Its contribution margin per order.
  - Customer acquisition cost.
  - How much the escrow float earned in interest.
  - How the take rate split between ads, subscriptions and fees.

## 6. Capital and financing

- **How the first capital was raised:** It came from Victor Fungkong, through PT Indonusa Dwitama.
  - Amount: about Rp2.4bn per the company deed (reported, Kontan) or Rp2.5bn per William (reported, Tempo). Both are about US$250k at the time.
  - Stake: about 80% to Indonusa (reported, Kontan); William had about 10% (reported, Tempo).
  - Expected return: Victor reportedly wanted the money back in 2-3 years.
  - In kind: Victor also provided office space at Patal Senayan, back-office staff and his own internet-business experience.
  - Conflict: William's interview says Victor provided capital in February 2008, while the company deed is February 2009. Possibly the commitment came first and the company later.
- **How growth was funded:** Almost entirely with outside equity.
  - Rounds in order: East Ventures (2010), CyberAgent (2011), BEENOS/Net Price (2012), SoftBank Ventures Korea (2013), US$100m from SoftBank and Sequoia (2014), about US$147m (2016, unconfirmed), US$1.1bn led by Alibaba (2017), US$1.1bn from SoftBank Vision Fund and Alibaba (2018), Google and Temasek (2020).
  - Total raised: about US$3.1bn by 2020 (reported, Global Venturing).
  - There is no evidence of meaningful retained earnings or debt funding growth.
- **Terms and structures worth noting:**
  - Preferred stock for VCs from the very first round (confirmed, 2010 press release). Preferred shareholders usually get paid back before common shareholders in a sale. That's why a founder's 10% of "headline valuation" is worth less than it looks (inference about the general structure; Tokopedia's exact terms are unknown).
  - A stock programme issuing new shares to top performers every six months (reported, Tempo).
  - A founder veto on acquisitions (reported, Tempo).
  - At GoTo: multiple-voting shares for founders. The Series B shares gave a small economic stake (about 6% in 2022) about 59% of the votes (reported, Samuel Sekuritas). That's how founders kept control at IPO despite owning little.
- **Closest thing to an acquisition playbook:** None as a buyer. He was on the selling side twice:
  - The GoTo merger: a share swap at about 58/42 (reported).
  - The TikTok deal: a primary share issue rather than a sale of existing shares, combined with an asset purchase and a promissory note. That structure was designed so that GoTo's remaining 24.99% would not be diluted by TikTok's future funding (confirmed, Kontan and Katadata).

  This is a useful example of how a controlling investor can enter by subscribing to new shares (saham baru) rather than buying from existing holders.

## 7. People and relationships

He was much less public than William. After the GoTo era he moved into politics: he was a co-captain of the Anies-Muhaimin campaign team in 2023, and was later appointed a deputy at a coordinating ministry (reported, detik and VOI). - **Mentors and patrons:** Victor Fungkong first. Masayoshi Son / SoftBank later: William credits an emergency 2014 Tokyo meeting, made possible because he already had a Japan visa for a planned proposal trip (reported, Nikkei via 36kr).
- **Key hires and delegation:**
  - Recruiting was hard at first. Two booths at a Binus job fair got zero applicants, so he switched to giving talks in small classes and waited one to two years for those students to graduate (reported, Tempo).
  - Patrick Cao became Tokopedia's president and then GoTo's president.
  - Melissa Siska Juminto led Tokopedia operations and later stayed on under TikTok (reported, CNBC ID).
  - His habit of creating "local leaders who are also shareholders" was a deliberate retention tool.
- **Government, bank, and conglomerate relationships:**
  - He received the Satyalancana Wira Karya from the President (confirmed, Kominfo).
  - He appointed Agus Martowardojo as chairman of commissioners in 2019.
  - He co-founded the Indonesian E-commerce Association (idEA) (reported, Wikipedia EN).
  - Public framing: "separate capital from control." In practice that meant foreign money with local faces, which helped with regulators and the public (inference).

His habit of writing open letters to users, for example after the 2020 breach, was part of maintaining that reputation.

## 8. Near-death moments and mistakes

1. **2007-2009: no money at all.** Two years of rejections while his father was seriously ill and he was the family's main earner (reported). It survived only because his boss funded it. Without Victor there is no Tokopedia. This was a relationship, not a market, saving the company.
2. **2010: global giants arrive.** eBay and Rakuten entered Indonesia, and US investors dismissed him. Survived by finding Japanese and Korean investors. The cost was that "raise big to survive" became the operating philosophy.
3. **2014: urgent need for money.** He reportedly needed funding urgently before the SoftBank/Sequoia round (reported, single source). It survived partly through luck (he already had the visa). The cost was more dilution and a shift to a subsidy-heavy growth strategy.
4. **2017-2023: the Shopee subsidy war.** Sea's Shopee and Lazada spent heavily on free shipping and cashback. Tokopedia's low take rate meant every promotion deepened losses. This ended with the GoTo merger and then the sale of control to TikTok. That was the real "death" of independence, even though the platform survived.
5. **May 2020: data breach.** A hacker offered up to 91m account records (names, emails, phone numbers, hashed passwords) for about US$5,000, and in July the data was shared for free (confirmed). Tokopedia said payment data was safe and that it was itself a victim (reported). It was a reputational, not a financial, near-death. It fed into the push for Indonesia's personal data protection law (UU PDP).
6. **Mistake (inference): monetization came very late.** Basic service was free until about 2015, and the take rate was still about 1-3% in 2021-2022, well below regional peers. A seller base used to paying nothing is hard to charge later without losing sellers to rivals.

## 9. Advantages, luck, and survivorship check

- **Head starts:**
  - Not family money: he explicitly listed the lack of a family safety net as a reason investors refused him.
  - But real advantages: a Jakarta IT degree; four years of professional engineering; working inside an internet-content company owned by someone with US startup experience; and being a man in tech in 2000s Jakarta.
  - Ethnicity: his Chinese-Indonesian background may have mattered in networks such as Victor and later investors, but no source says so directly (inference; do not over-weight).
- **Luck and timing:**
  - Mobile internet and smartphones spread across Indonesia in 2010-2015.
  - Yahoo's 2010 acquisition of Koprol put Indonesia on investors' maps (reported, William).
  - Global zero-interest-rate money, 2010-2021.
  - SoftBank's appetite in 2014 and the Japan visa coincidence.
  - The 2021-2022 IPO window.
  - Being the incumbent when the TikTok Shop ban made a local partner valuable.
- **What similar businesses that did not make it did differently:** Tokobagus stayed a classified site and was absorbed into OLX (to verify). Many smaller marketplaces raised too little to survive the subsidy war (inference). Bukalapak, the closest peer, also IPO'd and has reportedly since retreated from physical-goods marketplace (to verify). Lesson: in winner-take-most marketplaces, survival depended more on capital access than on product quality.
- **How much of this story is documented vs. founder myth:**
  - Documented: the warnet job, the funding rounds, the merger, the TikTok deal, and the 2023-2024 share trades.
  - Mostly his own telling: details of early hardships, the investor rejections, the "you were not born special" investor quote (CNBC 2021), and the visa story.
  - Underplayed in the popular story: "Warnet boy to unicorn" leaves out that his boss provided the money, office, back office, trademark and the company deed.

## 10. Indonesia-specific context

- **Market and regulatory conditions:**
  - Late 2000s: low card penetration and widespread bank transfers made escrow the critical trust layer.
  - Logistics: JNE and similar courier networks existed, but islands made delivery slow and expensive. Hence TokoCabang regional warehousing in 2019 and the Gojek delivery partnership in 2015.
  - Foreign ownership in e-commerce was permitted at scale, which allowed SoftBank and Alibaba money.
  - 2023: a trade ministry regulation banned transactions inside social media apps. That forced TikTok Shop to find a local licensed partner, which is what produced the Tokopedia deal.
- **What is different today:**
  - Cheap capital has gone. GoTo and the sector now push for profit.
  - E-commerce is consolidated around Shopee and TikTok-Tokopedia.
  - The UU PDP data protection law raises compliance costs.
  - As of early 2026 there were rumors that the Tokopedia app would be replaced by a standalone TikTok Shop app; TikTok denied this (reported, CNBC Indonesia, Feb 2026). About 420 Tokopedia staff had reportedly been laid off by Aug 2025.
  - A new marketplace founder today faces incumbents, not an empty market.
- **Cultural or relational factors an Indonesian operator should know:**
  - Your first investor is likely someone who has watched you work.
  - Senior establishment figures on the board of commissioners (dewan komisaris) are a standard credibility tool.
  - Nationalism matters: he timed the launch on 17 August, and the public cares about "local vs foreign control." He had to manage that perception repeatedly as foreign shareholders grew.

## 11. Criticisms and controversies

- **2020 data breach** (confirmed): up to 91m accounts exposed. It became a public case study for weak data-protection regulation. Lawsuits and consumer-group complaints were reported; their outcomes are unknown.
- **Counterfeit and prohibited goods:** Like all large open marketplaces, Tokopedia faced IP complaints. It signed an IP-protection cooperation with the Ministry of Law and Human Rights (reported, Antara). The scale of the problem is not documented here.
- **Foreign control:** It was repeatedly criticized as a "local unicorn" funded and ultimately controlled by Chinese and Japanese capital. William's answer was the capital-vs-control distinction. The 2024 TikTok takeover made the criticism concrete.
- **GoTo shareholder losses:** IPO buyers at Rp338 saw the price fall below Rp100 by late 2023 (William's Dec 2023 sales averaged about Rp92). Founders sold some shares near the lows, reportedly for personal needs (reported, Bisnis). This is not evidence of wrongdoing, but retail investors who bought into the founder story lost most of their money.
- **Layoffs** after the merger and under TikTok (reported).
- Proportionality: there are no documented allegations of fraud or legal wrongdoing against William personally in the sources reviewed.

## 12. Transferable lessons for Michael

**Steal:**
1. **Sell trust, not just product.** Tokopedia's escrow solved the one thing stopping buyers from paying. When you evaluate a Jakarta business, ask what customers fear (fake goods, late delivery, being cheated on credit or piutang). The owner who removes that fear can charge more.
2. **Recruit supply before launch.** The roughly 70 pre-signed sellers are the marketplace version of having anchor customers. For a distribution business, that means signing your first key accounts or principals before you commit capital.
3. **Use the cash cycle in your favor.** Customer pays first, supplier gets paid later, no inventory on your books. Tokopedia had a great cash cycle and still lost money, so a good cash cycle doesn't fix bad unit economics. Look for businesses with both.
4. **Learn the take-rate arithmetic.** It's a good way to practice reading financial statements: GTV, gross revenue, incentives, net revenue, contribution margin. GoTo's annual reports are on idx.co.id and can be your first real financial statements to work through.
5. **Use control rights, not just percentage ownership.** If you ever take partners or investors, negotiate vetoes on a sale, on major capex and on hiring or firing the CEO. In an Indonesian PT, these go in the anggaran dasar (articles of association) and shareholder agreement; confirm with a notaris and lawyer.

**Do not copy (and why):**
1. **Do not trade ownership for valuation.** He went from about 10% at founding to a small slice of GoTo, then watched TikTok take control. Your goal is cash flow you own. A "US$7bn" headline was worth about US$2bn when a real buyer showed up, and preferred shares get paid first.
2. **Do not give away the core product and hope to monetize later.** Free-then-charge only works with near-unlimited capital. A buy-and-operate business must be profitable per transaction from the start.
3. **Do not enter a subsidy war.** If your competitor can lose money longer than you can, you lose. Choose niches where capital isn't the deciding factor: local relationships, licences, B2B accounts, physical locations.

**Applies to buying an existing business:**
- In a seller's pitch, insist on a P&L and cash flow, not GTV, users or growth. Tokopedia-era metrics (GMV, MAU) hide losses.
- Check where money sits during the cash cycle. Who pays first? How many days of piutang and stock (persediaan)?
- Look for target businesses that sell to marketplace merchants: packaging, logistics, warehousing, accounting for UMKM. Those suppliers can earn steady margins while the platforms fight each other.
- The TikTok deal is a template for how a buyer can enter with new shares plus a funding commitment instead of a full buyout. Useful if a family business owner wants capital but not to sell everything.

**Applies to leveraging my software/AI background:**
- His engineering helped him build a product cheaply, and his SEO instinct got early traffic. Engineering didn't solve recruiting, fundraising, seller acquisition or monetization. Those were sales and relationship problems.
- A realistic edge for you: buy a traditional business, such as a distributor or a services company with many small customers, and add the "Tokopedia layer" yourself. That means online ordering, payment reconciliation, demand forecasting (as Tokopedia did for TokoCabang), and agent workflows for customer service. It's cheap for you to build and expensive for competitors.
- Don't assume tech skills transfer to managing non-tech staff. Tokopedia was itself a tech company, so this story teaches you little about that gap.

**Questions to ask a Jakarta business owner after reading this:**
1. "What share of your sales now go through Shopee, Tokopedia or TikTok Shop? What are your fees as a percentage, and how have they changed since 2023?"
2. "Who gets paid first in your business, you or your suppliers? How many days of receivables and stock do you carry?"
3. "If a bigger competitor started selling at a loss tomorrow, what would protect you?"
