## 1. One-paragraph summary

Kopi Kenangan is a grab-and-go coffee chain. Three friends opened it in 2017 with about Rp150 million of pooled money, in a small kiosk in a Kuningan office tower[^katadata-2022][^disway-2020]. The formula was Starbucks-quality milk coffee at about half the price (Rp18,000 a cup in 2020), from tiny stores with no sofas or wifi, rent capped at about 5% of sales, and capex capped at Rp500m per store[^disway-2020]. The first store reportedly paid back its capital in about 3 months[^republika-2020][^disway-2020]. That per-store profitability attracted about US$240m of venture capital (Alpha JWC, Sequoia India, Jay-Z's Arrive, Serena Ventures and others). The money paid for a rapid rollout, and the company reached unicorn status in Dec 2021[^dailysocial-2021][^avcj-2021]. Group-level losses followed: US$18.4m in 2023, narrowing to US$2.3m in 2024[^wcp-2026]. The company says it made its first full-year group profit in 2025, about US$17m on US$184m of revenue[^idnfin-2025][^e27-2026]. For you, this is the opposite case to Tokopedia: the unit is a physical store with knowable economics, close to what you'd buy and operate. The lesson is how disciplined unit economics and an app layer can be stacked into a chain, and what venture money does to that.

## 4. How the business actually makes money

- **Who pays, and for what:** Consumers buy coffee and other drinks, plus bread, chicken and cookies from sister brands[^prn-2021], in store or via delivery apps and Kopi Kenangan's own app. Delivery through ride-hailing apps (ojol) was about 60-70% of sales as of 2022[^katadata-2022].
- **Revenue model:** The company owns and runs its stores; Edward rejected franchising early. He argued a franchisor only books the fee, while an owner books the whole store's revenue[^disway-2020]. Abroad it later franchises in the Philippines and Australia, while owning stores in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and India (reported)[^itiger-2026].
- **Unit economics, using the 2020 founder numbers**[^disway-2020]:
  - Price: Rp18,000 a cup (Rp20,000 with tax). The logic: a daily drink must not eat a large share of a minimum-wage salary. Roadside coffee is about Rp3,000 and chain coffee about Rp40,000.
  - Inputs: Greenfields fresh milk (milk is about 60% of the cup), Sukabumi palm sugar, and an Arabica-Robusta blend.
  - Store: 50 m² maximum. Rent capped at about 5% of the store's revenue. Capex capped at Rp500m: Rp200m for an Italian espresso machine, Rp300m for everything else. No sofas, no wifi.
  - Worked example (inference, my arithmetic): 230 stores selling 3m cups a month[^disway-2020] is about 435 cups per store per day. At Rp18,000 that's about Rp7.8m a day, or about Rp235m a month per store. Rent at 5% is about Rp12m a month. Recovering Rp500m of capex in about 3-4 months would need roughly Rp125-165m a month of store-level profit, about 50-70% of revenue. That is implausibly high once ingredients, staff and delivery commissions are included.
  - So either the first store had much lower capex (it used Rp150m total[^katadata-2022], less than one Rp200m machine; the two numbers don't reconcile), or "payback in 3 months" was measured on early capital at an exceptionally high-traffic store. **Treat the 3-month payback as marketing until you see store P&Ls.**
- **Unit economics, using the 2025 company numbers (inference):**
  - Revenue of about US$184m[^e27-2026] over an average of about 1,150 stores (977 at end-2024, 1,324 at end-2025)[^idxch-2026] is about US$160k per store per year, roughly Rp2.6bn, or Rp7m a day.
  - At an assumed average ticket of Rp25,000 (unsourced assumption; actual unknown), that's about 280 cups a day. That's lower than 2019's 435, which is plausible as stores move from prime Jakarta sites into smaller cities.
  - Group EBITDA margin about 20% (37/184), net margin about 9% (17/184)[^e27-2026]. Edward also cited an Indonesia net margin of 18-20%[^foodbiz-2025], which doesn't reconcile with group figures; it may use a different definition.
- **Cost structure and the main cost driver:**
  - Ingredients, chiefly milk[^disway-2020]. Store staff (7,521 jobs, Rp467bn of wages in 2025)[^fortune-ipo-2026]. Rent (capped by rule). Delivery-platform commissions; the exact rate is unknown, and about 20% is typical in Indonesia (unsourced, verify). Marketing.
  - Lower marketing spend was a key driver of the 2024 improvement[^dsa-2024].
  - The main cost driver is likely ingredients plus delivery commissions (inference). With 60-70% of sales through ojol[^katadata-2022], the platforms take a large cut of gross margin. That explains the push to the company's own app: 1.5m monthly transacting users by Dec 2025 (up 116%) and 4.47m new digital customers in 2025[^idnfin-2025-id].
- **Cash cycle:** Customers pay at once, in cash, by e-wallet, or via the app; delivery platforms pay out on their own settlement cycle (unknown). Suppliers are likely paid on terms (unknown). Inventory is perishable and turns fast. So working capital needs are low and the cash cycle is probably favorable (inference). The capital need is store capex, which is why each funding round maps to a store-opening plan[^dailysocial-2018][^idxch-2026].
- **Distribution and customer acquisition:**
  - Office-tower and mall kiosks at first, where Edward saw queues for coffee at the Jakarta stock exchange building[^disway-2020].
  - Viral social media and memorable naming: "Kopi Kenangan Mantan" (coffee of an ex-lover's memories)[^bisnis-2019][^wiki-en].
  - Ojol delivery[^katadata-2022] and the company's own app with Kenangan VIP loyalty tiers[^wiki-en].
  - Speed of rollout, about one new store a day[^kumparan-2019][^wiki-en].
- **Moat or unfair advantage:** Weak per product: palm-sugar milk coffee was popularized by Kopi Tuku in 2015[^wiki-en], and anyone can copy it. The real advantages are:
  - Site selection and opening speed.
  - Purchasing scale (milk, beans).
  - App data and loyalty.
  - Brand. It's in the top two of Indonesia's Top Brand Index alongside Janji Jiwa[^upn-tbi], and was most-liked in a 2025 GoodStats survey (42%)[^umn-goodstats].
  - Capital to outlast rivals (inference).
- **What is unknown here:**
  - Store-level P&L and the payback period at today's stores.
  - Average ticket.
  - Delivery commission rates and delivery's share of sales today.
  - Gross margin.
  - Store closure rate.
  - Whether the 2025 profit is audited. It was described as unaudited[^idnfin-2025-jp].

## 6. Capital and financing

- **How the first capital was raised:** About Rp150m pooled among the founders (patungan)[^katadata-2022][^kumparan-2019]. Edward said the largest item was the espresso machine (La Marzocco or Victoria Arduino)[^okezone-2020]. There's no record of loans for the first store (unknown).
- **How growth was funded:** Mostly cash flow from early stores at first, then VC.
  - Seed: US$8m (Oct 2018, Alpha JWC).
  - Series A: US$20m (Jun 2019, Sequoia India and Alpha JWC).
  - Series A+: about US$1.5m (Dec 2019; Arrive, Serena Ventures, Caris LeVert, Jonathan Neman, Sequoia India).
  - Series B: US$109m (May 2020, led by Sequoia India with B Capital, Horizons, Verlinvest, Kunlun, Sofina and Alpha JWC).
  - Series C first close: US$96m (Dec 2021, led by Tybourne with Horizons, Kunlun, B Capital and Falcon Edge).
  - Total about US$240m[^dailysocial-2021][^avcj-2021][^kontan-seriesB].
  - B Capital co-founder Eduardo Saverin joined the board after Series B[^avcj-2021].
  - Debt financing: none found (unknown).
- **Terms and structures worth noting:**
  - Founder stakes after about US$240m of VC are undisclosed.
  - Edward's Sep 2026 purchases from early employees show founders can claw back ownership later through secondary deals[^insideretail-2026][^retailnews-2026]. He framed it as letting early team members, who joined at 8 stores, get liquidity[^insideretail-2026].
  - The Common, a separate leasing company, sublets extra store space[^disway-2020]. It's a simple real-estate structure worth copying (check the tax and lease implications with a notaris and tax advisor).
  - The 2024 numbers came from a filing with Singapore's ACRA[^wcp-2026][^dsa-2024], which suggests a Singapore holding company above the Indonesian PT (inference; confirm the structure).
- **Acquisition playbook:** None. All growth has been organic, through new stores and new brands.

## 8. Near-death moments and mistakes

1. **Coal bust (about 2014-2015, before Kopi Kenangan).** The family business failed and assets were sold to repay the bank[^katadata-2022][^jis]. It shaped his rule of only entering businesses with price control.
2. **Lewis & Carroll struggled.** The premium tea concept didn't fit budget-conscious youth[^compasslist], and a fifth store underperformed (low-reliability source)[^marketrealist]. The lesson he took was about affordability and delivery[^dsa-speaker].
3. **COVID 2020.** About 70% of outlets closed or restricted[^idxch-2020]. It survived because office traffic shifted to delivery and the app (Fortune IDN reports app new users up almost 500% from Nov 2019 to 2020)[^fortune-pandemic], and because a US$109m Series B closed in May 2020[^kontan-seriesB].
4. **Post-unicorn losses (2021-2024).** Rapid domestic and overseas expansion; 2023 net loss about US$18.4m[^wcp-2026]. Fixed by cutting marketing and tightening costs, with the loss down to US$2.3m in 2024[^dsa-2024]. A Tiger Brokers summary describes five years of losses after the pandemic (reported)[^itiger-2026].
5. **Contrast case, Flash Coffee.** A VC-backed, delivery-first rival closed all Singapore stores in Oct 2023, reportedly owing staff wages, then shrank to an Indonesia-only business[^bisnis-flash][^dsa-flash]. Analysts said its strategy was wrong from the start[^bisnis-flash2]. Kopi Kenangan's survival should be read against chains that tried the same playbook and failed.
6. **Possible mistake (inference): heavy dependence on delivery platforms**, at 60-70% of sales in 2022[^katadata-2022], gave platforms leverage over margins. The app push is the response; how far it has reduced that dependence is unknown.

## 9. Advantages, luck, and survivorship check

- **Head starts:**
  - A US finance degree[^dsa-speaker], Big Four experience[^disway-2020], and an elite international school background. Dahlan names SMA Pelita Harapan; a JIS community page features him as an alumnus[^jis]. The two conflict; verify.
  - A business family. Even after the coal collapse, the network and the ability to pool Rp150m are not "from zero." Dahlan's readers made this point bluntly in the comments, which are not a source.
  - A bean supplier relationship with Tuku's founder[^disway-2020].
- **Luck and timing:**
  - The "kopi kekinian" wave was just starting. Tuku had popularized palm-sugar milk coffee in 2015[^wiki-en], and Edward said timing was key because the market wasn't crowded yet[^teknologi-id].
  - Ojol delivery was growing fast[^katadata-2022].
  - Gojek's success had made Indonesia investable for foreign VCs[^disway-2020].
  - The Series B closed in May 2020, in the middle of COVID[^kontan-seriesB].
  - The 2021 easy-money peak for the unicorn round.
- **What similar businesses that did not make it did differently:**
  - Flash Coffee: expanded across many countries at once on a delivery-first model, then collapsed outside Indonesia[^bisnis-flash][^the-ken-flash].
  - Survivors and rivals: Janji Jiwa, which franchised widely (unsourced, verify), Fore (listed on IDX in 2025)[^cnbcid-fore][^idnfin-ipo-2026], Tomoro, and Point Coffee (Indomaret, 1,200+ outlets by end-2024, low-reliability source)[^umn-point].
  - Lesson: format discipline (small stores, rent rules) plus one dominant home market seems to beat multi-country sprawl (inference).
- **How much of this story is documented vs. founder myth:**
  - Documented: the funding rounds (multiple outlets), store counts, the 2024 ACRA-based figures and the 2026 share purchases.
  - Founder claims: the 3-month payback, the 2019 profitability and the 2025 numbers (unaudited). Dahlan Iskan's column is a friendly profile.

## 10. Indonesia-specific context

- **Market and regulatory conditions:**
  - A huge price gap between roadside coffee (about Rp3,000) and international chains (about Rp40,000)[^disway-2020].
  - Office-worker density in Jakarta CBDs.
  - The ojol ecosystem made delivery possible at scale.
  - Indonesia is a major coffee producer, so local beans are available (all of Kopi Kenangan's beans are Indonesian[^cnbcid-ipo-2026]).
  - Halal status matters for a mass-market brand; Kopi Kenangan is certified (low-reliability source[^zerog]; verify via BPJPH).
- **What is different today:**
  - The market is crowded: Point Coffee, Tomoro, Fore and Janji Jiwa, plus Chinese entrants. Mixue is widely reported (unsourced, verify); M Stand entered Jakarta in Oct 2026[^wcp-mstand].
  - Fore, a listed peer, gives public comparables: FY2025 net profit about Rp90bn vs Kopi Kenangan's claimed about US$17m[^idnfin-ipo-2026].
  - Prime sites are taken. New stores go to smaller cities: 141 cities per the company site in Oct 2026[^investorid-2026].
- **Cultural or relational factors an Indonesian operator should know:**
  - Naming and emotional branding ("mantan," memories) travel well on social media[^bisnis-2019][^wiki-en].
  - The no-layoff pledge resonated publicly.
  - Families and friends pooling money (patungan) is the normal way first stores get funded.

## 11. Criticisms and controversies

- **"Not from zero":** commenters and the coal-family background suggest the founders had more privilege than the Rp150m story implies[^katadata-2022]. Proportionate point: Rp150m is real money, but it's small relative to the family's previous business scale.
- **Unverified profitability claims:** the 2019 "already profitable" claim[^detik-2019b] sits awkwardly with later reports of losses[^wcp-2026][^itiger-2026]. The 2025 profit is unaudited[^idnfin-2025-jp]. Figures also differ across outlets: 2024 revenue is US$119m per the filing coverage[^wcp-2026] vs US$127m per IDX Channel[^idxch-2026], and 2025 profit is US$17m or US$18m[^e27-2026][^fortune-ipo-2026].
- **Environmental:** millions of plastic cups a month; raised by commenters, no formal controversy found.

## 12. Transferable lessons for Michael

**Steal:**
1. **Write unit rules before you scale.** Max 50 m², rent at most about 5% of sales, capex at most Rp500m per unit[^disway-2020]. For any multi-unit business you buy (clinics, laundromats, minimarts, F&B), define the rules a new unit must pass before opening.
2. **Pick businesses where you set the price.** This was Edward's lesson from coal[^katadata-2022]. When you screen acquisitions, avoid businesses that are price-takers on their main input or output, or hedge it.
3. **Price for the daily budget of your actual customer.** Rp18,000 vs a minimum-wage salary was the whole strategy[^disway-2020].
4. **Own the customer relationship.** With 60-70% of sales through platforms[^katadata-2022], an app and loyalty programme (1.5m monthly transacting users[^idnfin-2025-id]) is margin defence. This is your software edge.
5. **Use a separate company to manage leases and sublets** (The Common)[^disway-2020].

**Do not copy (and why):**
1. **Don't trust headline payback claims.** The 3-month payback doesn't reconcile with the capex numbers (section 4). Ask for store-level P&Ls.
2. **Don't take VC unless you need speed against competitors.** The money produced the 2023 loss and undisclosed dilution. Edward later had to buy shares back[^insideretail-2026].
3. **Don't expand to many countries before the home market is locked.** Flash Coffee is the cautionary case[^bisnis-flash].

**Applies to buying an existing business:**
- If you buy a single F&B or retail unit or a small chain, Kopi Kenangan's metrics are your diligence checklist:
  - Cups or transactions per day.
  - Average ticket.
  - Rent as % of sales.
  - Same-store sales growth (Kopi Kenangan: about 15% in Indonesia in 2025[^idnfin-2025-id]).
  - Delivery share and commission.
  - Capex per unit and payback.
- Buying a franchisee or a small regional chain of a known format could be a lower-risk entry than starting a brand.
- Listed peer Fore files public reports on IDX. Use them to learn real F&B chain margins before you trust any seller's numbers.

**Applies to leveraging my software/AI background:**
- The "new retail" layer means app ordering, loyalty, demand data and site selection. Kopi Kenangan's founders are finance people who hired for it[^dailysocial-2018].
- You could bring this layer to a traditional chain you buy: an ordering app, WhatsApp ordering, demand forecasting for perishables, and staff scheduling. That is a real edge.

**Questions to ask a Jakarta business owner after reading this:**
1. "What is your rent as a percentage of sales at each location, and which locations are above 10%?"
2. "What share of your sales come through GoFood, GrabFood or ShopeeFood, and what do they charge you?"
3. "How much did your last unit cost to open, and how many months did it take to earn that back? Can I see that unit's monthly P&L?"
